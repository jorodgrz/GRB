\begin{abstract}
Short and long gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) with kilonovae both arise from compact
binary mergers, but the central engine that launches the jet remains
unidentified. \citet{gottlieb2023,gottlieb2024} argue that the relevant divide
is not burst duration but the merger remnant: a hyper-massive neutron star
(HMNS) or a black-hole accretion disk. The engine mix is then a prediction of
binary evolution. We test it by classifying 20 population-synthesis models by
central engine, weighting every statistic by the simulations' importance
weights, and integrating each class's metallicity-dependent merger rate to
$z = 10$. In the fiducial model the binary neutron star (BNS) engine mix is
close to even, long-duration engines taking $53\%$ of the weighted population
against a $47\%$ short-duration, blue-kilonova class that nonetheless supplies
$69\%$ of the local merger rate, and $82\%$ of black hole neutron star (BHNS)
mergers swallow the neutron star and produce no burst. The intrinsic local BNS
rate, $47\,\Gpcyr$, is consistent with GWTC-5.0; the BHNS rate, $73\,\Gpcyr$,
exceeds the upper edge of the GWTC-5.0 NSBH interval by a factor of $\sim 2.2$. The observed
kilonova-bearing bursts invert the naive duration-to-engine mapping: the ejecta
of the long-duration events are consistent with a black-hole-disk origin, while
the short-duration events carry $1.65$ dex (a factor of $\sim 45$) more ejecta
than the long ones and require HMNS-wind enrichment. Across the grid the local
rate spans two orders of magnitude and the engine mix turns over with it: long-duration
engines hold the majority in 13 of the 20 variations and the short-duration,
blue-kilonova class in the remaining 7, reaching $85\%$ where mass transfer is
held at a fixed efficiency. The engine demographics are therefore a prediction
of the binary physics rather than a fixed output of the merger channel, and the
growing sample of engine-classified bursts, not the event count, is the sharper
test of the remnant picture. All disk-mass-based GRB rates assume jet launching in every
above-threshold system and are upper limits.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}
%% ======================================================================
Compact binary mergers that contain at least one neutron star (NS) power both
gravitational-wave (GW) transients and a rich set of electromagnetic
counterparts. Neutron-star mergers were proposed as the progenitors of short
gamma-ray bursts (sGRBs) decades before the first multi-messenger detection
\citep{paczynski1986,eichler1989,narayan1992}, and the same mergers were
predicted to power radioactively driven kilonovae from their r-process ejecta
\citep{lipaczynski1998,metzger2010,metzger2019}. As the multi-band sample has
grown, this picture has expanded to include a population of longer-duration
bursts accompanied by red kilonovae \citep{rastinejad2024,levan2024}. The joint
detection of GW170817, GRB\,170817A, and AT2017gfo confirmed the binary neutron
star (BNS) channel as an sGRB and kilonova progenitor
\citep{abbott2017mma,abbott2019gw170817,goldstein2017,kasen2017},
and the second confirmed BNS GW190425 \citep{abbott2020gw190425} extended the
known mass range. A central open question is which central engine launches the
relativistic jet and sets the composition of the kilonova ejecta.

\citet{gottlieb2023,gottlieb2024} proposed that the merger remnant determines two physically distinct engine branches. A black-hole (BH) accretion disk, formed either promptly in a BNS merger or after NS tidal disruption in a black hole neutron star (BHNS) binary, launches a jet
whose energetics scale with the disk mass. A long-lived hyper-massive neutron
star (HMNS) or magnetar remnant instead drives a magnetically dominated wind
that both extends the burst duration and enriches the ejecta. The two branches
predict different durations, ejecta masses, and kilonova colors, and therefore
admit a population-level test once a synthetic compact-binary population is
classified and its cosmic rate computed.

\subsection{Neutron-star mass treatment} \label{sec:remap}

Both \compas\ supernova engines inherit a $\sim 1.7\,\Msun$ deficit in the
neutron-star mass distribution from the \citet{fryer2012} baryonic-to-gravitational
mass conversion. We take the raw \compas\ masses as the fiducial catalog: they are
the self-consistent output of the binary-evolution calculation, and no post-hoc
statistical transform can supply support that the simulation never populated.

Two consequences of that choice need care. First, \compas\ types a remnant as a
neutron star up to its run-time $M_{\rm NS,max}$ (2.5, 2.0, or $3.0\,\Msun$ across
the grid), a configuration knob unrelated to and larger than the cold-stability
limit $\Mtov = 2.2\,\Msun$ we adopt \citep{raaijmakers2021}. A ``neutron star''
heavier than \Mtov\ cannot exist as a cold, non-rotating star under that equation of
state, so a binary carrying such a component is not a BNS and must leave the sample
before classification, which tests only $\Mtot$ and $q$. We therefore apply a
cold-stability cut, retaining systems whose every component satisfies
$m_i \leq \Mtov$, and report the discarded weight fraction; for Model A the cut
removes a small tail of super-\Mtov\ ``neutron stars'', and the fraction grows with
$M_{\rm NS,max}$ across the grid. The cut moves with the EOS in the sensitivity study
below, so the retained sample is always internally consistent with the assumed \Mtov.

Second, the empirical double-Gaussian mass function of \citet{alsing2018},
\begin{equation}
f(m) \propto W_1\,\mathcal{N}(m; \mu_1, \sigma_1) + W_2\,\mathcal{N}(m; \mu_2, \sigma_2),
\label{eq:nsremap}
\end{equation}
with $(W_1, \mu_1, \sigma_1) = (0.66, 1.34\,\Msun, 0.07\,\Msun)$ and
$(W_2, \mu_2, \sigma_2) = (0.34, 1.80\,\Msun, 0.21\,\Msun)$, has been used to remap
synthetic NS masses onto a Galactic-like distribution by mapping each weighted input
mass through the empirical CDF onto the quantile of this target (truncated to
$[1.10,\ \Mtov]\,\Msun$). The $1.65$ to $1.80\,\Msun$ interval is not empty in
\compas\ (it holds a few percent of the weight, with a narrow genuinely-empty void at
$[1.70,\ 1.76)\,\Msun$); the remap inflates the weight there (by roughly a factor of
$1.6$ on Model A), so it fabricates rather than recovers support in the Fryer deficit.
We retain it only as a labelled systematic axis
(a mass-treatment $\times$ EOS sensitivity grid), not on the science path; the physically
motivated recipe of \citet{mandel2020} acts inside the binary-evolution step rather
than as this kind of post-hoc quantile transform, so it motivates concern about the
deficit but does not license the remap as a fiducial correction.

The void is specific to the delayed prescription rather than to the
baryonic-to-gravitational conversion that all the models apply: the rapid engine of
Model L populates $[1.70,\ 1.76)\,\Msun$ with $812$ systems, where the delayed models
leave it exactly empty. The delayed-versus-rapid comparison therefore brackets the
remnant-prescription systematic entirely within self-consistent binary evolution, which
is what the quantile transform cannot do. That comparison is not a clean isolation of
the void, since the rapid engine also cools the whole NS mass function (the weight above
$1.6\,\Msun$ falls from $21.3$ to $3.3$ per cent), but every mass entering it is one the
population-synthesis calculation actually assigned.

\subsection{Remnant and ejecta physics} \label{sec:remnant}

The BHNS classification rests on the post-merger accretion-disk mass, which we
assemble from a chain of numerical-relativity fitting formulae. The neutron-star
radius follows a phenomenological mass-radius relation anchored at
$R_{1.4} \approx 12$ km \citep{raaijmakers2021}, with a cubic softening toward $\Mtov$,
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm NS}(M) = R_{1.4}\left[1 - 0.15\left(\frac{M - 1.4\,\Msun}{\Mtov - 1.4\,\Msun}\right)^{3}\right],
\label{eq:nsradius}
\end{equation}
held flat at $R_{1.4}$ below $1.4\,\Msun$ and undefined above $\Mtov$. This is a
modeling choice rather than an equation-of-state derivation. The neutron-star
compactness is then
\begin{equation}
C = \frac{G M_{\rm NS}}{R_{\rm NS}\,c^2},
\label{eq:compactness}
\end{equation}
and the baryonic mass follows the quadratic approximation
\begin{equation}
M_b = M_{\rm NS} + 0.080\,M_{\rm NS}^2
\label{eq:baryon}
\end{equation}
\citep{gao2020,lattimer2001}, with masses in $\Msun$. For a Kerr black hole of dimensionless spin
$\chi \equiv \aBH$, the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) radius in units of
$G M_{\rm BH}/c^2$ is \citep{bardeen1972}
\begin{align}
Z_1 &= 1 + (1-\chi^2)^{1/3}\!\left[(1+\chi)^{1/3} + (1-\chi)^{1/3}\right], \nonumber\\
Z_2 &= \sqrt{3\chi^2 + Z_1^2}, \label{eq:isco}\\
\hat{r}_{\rm ISCO} &= 3 + Z_2 - \mathrm{sgn}(\chi)\sqrt{(3-Z_1)(3+Z_1+2Z_2)}, \nonumber
\end{align}
taking the prograde root for $\chi \geq 0$ and the retrograde root for $\chi < 0$.

The baryon mass left outside the black hole after tidal disruption is the
\citet[their Eq.~4]{foucart2018} fit
\begin{equation}
\frac{M_{\rm rem}}{M_b} = \left[\max\!\left(0,\ \alpha\,\frac{1-2C}{\eta^{1/3}}
- \beta\,\hat{r}_{\rm ISCO}\,\frac{C}{\eta} + \gamma\right)\right]^{\delta},
\label{eq:foucart}
\end{equation}
with symmetric mass ratio $\eta = M_{\rm NS} M_{\rm BH}/(M_{\rm NS}+M_{\rm BH})^2$,
mass ratio $Q \equiv M_{\rm BH}/M_{\rm NS}$, and best-fit coefficients
$(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta) = (0.406, 0.139, 0.255, 1.761)$. The fit is
calibrated for $Q \in [1, 7]$, $\chi \in [-0.5, 0.9]$, and $C \in [0.13, 0.182]$.
We clip spins at $\chi = 0.9$ to stay inside the calibrated spin range; the
mass-ratio range has no such guard, and 63 percent of the merging BHNS sample
(43 percent by weight) has $Q > 7$, extending to $Q = 29.4$, where
Eq.~\ref{eq:foucart} is an extrapolation. The extrapolation is benign for the
classification because the fit predicts essentially no remnant mass at these
mass ratios. By weight, 57 percent of the No-GRB class sits at $Q > 7$,
against only 0.2 percent of the faint-lbGRB class and none of the lbGRB plus
red kilonova class; forcing every $Q > 7$ system into the No-GRB class would
change the class fractions by at most 0.05 percentage points. The compactness
guard is benign in the same way: the fit is calibrated for $C \in [0.13, 0.182]$
and neutron stars near $\Mtov$ exceed the upper edge, but a higher compactness
is a more tightly bound star that resists tidal disruption, so it leaves no disk
and defaults to the No-GRB class, and the extrapolation cannot manufacture
spurious GRB-producing systems.
The dynamical ejecta follow \citet[their Eq.~9]{kruger2020},
\begin{equation}
\frac{M_{\rm dyn}}{M_b} = \max\!\left(0,\ a_1 Q^{n_1}\frac{1-2C}{C}
- a_2 Q^{n_2}\hat{r}_{\rm ISCO} + a_4\right),
\label{eq:kfdyn}
\end{equation}
with $(a_1, a_2, a_4) = (0.007116, 0.001436, -0.02762)$ and
$(n_1, n_2) = (0.8636, 1.6840)$. The BHNS accretion-disk mass is the difference
\begin{equation}
\Mdisk = \max\!\left(0,\ M_{\rm rem} - M_{\rm dyn}\right).
\label{eq:disk}
\end{equation}
For a spin misaligned from the orbital angular momentum by a tilt angle
$\theta_{\rm tilt}$, only the aligned projection enters the fit through the
effective spin $\chi_{\rm eff} = \max(0,\ \aBH\cos\theta_{\rm tilt})$.

Neutron-star radii
in the fiducial disk-mass chain use the heuristic of Eq.~\ref{eq:nsradius} at
$R_{1.4} \approx 12$ km; the APR4, SFHo, LS220, and DD2 tabulations of
\citet{bauswein2013,bauswein2020} and \citet{read2009} supply the EOS-dependent
critical masses (and EOS-anchored radii where an EOS is named explicitly).

\subsection{GRB classification} \label{sec:classification}

We adopt the \citet{gottlieb2024} four-class hybrid scheme for BNS. With
$\Mtov = 2.2\,\Msun$ \citep{raaijmakers2021}, the prompt-collapse and
hyper-massive-neutron-star (HMNS) boundaries are
\begin{align}
\Mthresh &= k\,\Mtov = 1.27 \times 2.2\,\Msun = 2.794\,\Msun, \nonumber \\
M_{\rm HMNS} &= 1.2\,\Mtov = 2.64\,\Msun,
\label{eq:thresholds}
\end{align}
at the \citet{gottlieb2023} fiducial $k = 1.27$, with mass ratio
$q \equiv m_1/m_2 \geq 1$ and $q_{\rm thresh} = 1.2$. The four classes are
then defined on the total mass $\Mtot = m_1 + m_2$ and $q$; the full decision
tree is summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:scheme}.
The prompt-collapse threshold concept is from \citet{bauswein2013}, who give an
EOS-dependent $k \in [1.30, 1.70]$; the value $k = 1.27$ is a \citet{gottlieb2023}
fiducial chosen so that $\Mthresh \approx 2.8\,\Msun$. The long-lived HMNS split at
$M_{\rm HMNS} = 1.2\,\Mtov$ is a code heuristic motivated by the maximum mass that
uniform rotation can support \citep{margalit2017}, not a value tabulated by
\citet{gottlieb2024}. The equation of state enters only through $\Mtov$ and this
fiducial $k$; we adopt a single EOS-motivated $(\Mtov, k)$ pair throughout rather
than sweeping the EOS. Table~\ref{tab:params} collects the key physical
parameters of the classification, the rate corrections of
Section~\ref{sec:corrections}, and the observed-sample engine comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:obsmethod}, separating each fiducial value from the range
swept or marginalized.

For BHNS we use three disk-mass classes with thresholds $0.01$ and $0.1\,\Msun$
\citep{gottlieb2023}, shown in the right branch of Figure~\ref{fig:scheme}. The
class names follow the \citet{gottlieb2024} hybrid so they align with the BNS
labels. A unified mass-plane classifier assigns integer labels on the
$(m_1, m_2)$ plane for the diagnostic figures, truncating the BNS region at the
model-specific $M_{\rm NS,max}$.

\subsection{Post-supernova kinematics and host-galaxy offsets} \label{sec:kinematics}

Each binary that survives both supernovae recoils with a systemic
center-of-mass velocity $v_{\rm sys}$, built up from the natal kicks and from
the \citet{blaauw1961} recoil of instantaneous mass loss; \compas\ reports
$v_{\rm sys}$ for every merging system. Combined with the delay time $t_d$,
this velocity fixes where the merger occurs relative to its host galaxy. The
ballistic estimate $v_{\rm sys}\,t_d$ ignores the host potential and, at the
Gyr delay times common in the population, predicts offsets of $10^2$ to
$10^3$ kpc for systems that are in fact bound to their hosts, so we instead
integrate each system through a host potential, following the bound-orbit
treatment of \citet{bloom1999}.

Hosts are modeled as \citet{hernquist1990} spheres,
\begin{equation}
\Phi(r) = -\frac{G\,M_{\rm gal}}{r + a}, \qquad a = R_e / 1.8153,
\label{eq:hernquist}
\end{equation}
with stellar mass $M_{\rm gal}$ and scale radius $a$ set by the projected
half-light radius $R_e$. We use the projected Hernquist relation rather than
the three-dimensional half-mass radius $r_{1/2} = (1+\sqrt{2})\,a$ because
observed effective radii from S\'ersic fits are projected quantities. The
stellar sphere is embedded in a \citet{navarro1997} dark-matter halo whose
mass follows from the host stellar mass through the \citet{behroozi2013}
stellar-to-halo relation (their Eq.~3, inverted numerically) and whose
concentration follows \citet{dutton2014}; the halo is built at the median
measured redshift of the classical comparison sample. The total potential is
the sum of the two components, and reduces to the stellar-only Hernquist form
when the halo mass is set to zero. Birth
sites trace the stellar light: inverting the Hernquist enclosed-mass fraction
$M(<r)/M_{\rm gal} = r^2/(r+a)^2$ gives the launch radius
\begin{equation}
r_0 = a\,\frac{\sqrt{u}}{1 - \sqrt{u}}, \qquad u \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 1).
\label{eq:birthradius}
\end{equation}
The post-supernova drift is followed in two legs, because \compas\ records a
systemic speed after each of the binary's two supernovae and the system spends
most of its pre-merger life at the second of them; integrating the whole delay
time at the post-second-supernova speed would carry the binary at that speed
through the tens of Myr it spent as an ordinary star before the first explosion.
Before the first supernova the binary orbits in equilibrium, so its position
then is a fresh draw from Equation~\eqref{eq:birthradius} with a pre-kick
velocity drawn per Cartesian component from $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_r(r_0))$. The
dispersion $\sigma_r(r)$ is the isotropic solution of the spherical Jeans
equation for the Hernquist tracer density in the total potential, so the
population is launched in equilibrium; solving it in the total rather than the
stellar-only potential matters because with a halo present the Hernquist
analytic dispersion would start the stars out of equilibrium and they would
relax outward over the integration, a spurious expansion that grows with delay
time. The first leg adds the post-first-supernova systemic velocity as an
isotropic kick \citep{bloom1999} and integrates for the interval between the two
supernovae; the second leg reassigns the centre-of-mass velocity to a fresh
isotropic vector whose magnitude is the cumulative post-second-supernova speed
\compas\ reports, adds a freshly drawn ambient dispersion, and integrates for
the gravitational-wave inspiral time. Each leg conserves its specific angular
momentum and stays in a plane, and is advanced in that plane in Cartesian
coordinates by a symplectic velocity-Verlet integrator with the step count set
from the local orbital period. Cartesian integration avoids the centrifugal
singularity of the reduced radial equation for the near-radial orbits isotropic
kicks produce, and the symplectic scheme keeps the per-orbit energy error
bounded rather than drifting with orbit count, which matters because bound
systems complete hundreds of revolutions over Gyr inspiral times and orbit
count is monotonic in delay time, the very axis along which the class offsets
differ. The phase cap is applied only to bound orbits: a bound orbit randomizes
its radial phase within a few tens of radial periods, so a bound system whose
drift time exceeds forty radial periods is integrated over that capped,
phase-randomized baseline, which also removes any residual delay-time
correlation in the integration error. An unbound system, identified by
comparing its launch speed to the local escape speed in the total potential, has
no radial period to mix and is integrated for its full drift time, completing
ballistically once it has left the host and is nearly free; this keeps the
wide-offset tail, where a few percent of each class lies beyond the plotted
axis, physical rather than truncated at the cap. The projected offset is
$R_{\rm off} = r(t_d)\,(1 - \mu^2)^{1/2}$ for a single isotropic viewing angle
$\mu$ drawn per system, so each modeled offset is one realization directly
comparable to the single measured offset of an observed burst; all random draws
use a fixed seed for reproducibility.

The host population is a fixed three-template mixture: a star-forming disk
($\log_{10} M_{\rm gal}/\Msun = 9.8$, $R_e = 3.6$ kpc), a massive
star-forming galaxy ($10.5$, $5.0$ kpc), and an elliptical ($11.0$, $8.0$
kpc), combined with mixture weights $0.50/0.25/0.25$. The 75/25 split between
star-forming and elliptical hosts follows the sGRB host demographics of
\citet{fong2013}; the template stellar masses bracket the sGRB host
stellar-mass distribution of \citet{leibler2010}, whose star-forming-host
median the larger uniformly modeled sample of \citet{nugent2022} confirms;
and the effective radii follow the HST S\'ersic medians of \citet{fong2013}
and \citet{fong2022}. The finer 50/25 division between the two star-forming
templates is a modeling choice, not a measured fraction. Each class
population is subsampled by a simple random draw without replacement to at most
$2 \times 10^4$ systems, retaining the original STROOPWAFEL weights, which is
unbiased for any weighted functional; every system is then integrated in all
three potentials, and the mixture weights multiply the STROOPWAFEL weights in
the combined offset distribution.
The templates are spherical; the dark-matter halo raises the escape speed and
binds systems that a stellar-only potential would let escape, so the
high-offset tails are physical predictions rather than upper envelopes. The
residual excess of the model tails over the observed distributions is a
selection effect, since the observed samples are afterglow plus host
selected.

Model-to-data offset comparisons use a Monte-Carlo-calibrated one-sample
Anderson-Darling (AD) goodness-of-fit test. The STROOPWAFEL model sample is
roughly three orders of magnitude larger than any observed offset catalogue, so
the comparison is a one-sample problem: the weighted model distribution is the
reference and the observed offsets are the sample tested against it. AD rather
than Kolmogorov-Smirnov because the discriminating power for host offsets sits
in the large-offset tail, where the KS supremum statistic is least sensitive
\citep[the same choice made by][]{skobe2026}. Each observed offset $x_j$ is
mapped to $u_j = \hat{F}(x_j)$ through the weighted model CDF
$\hat{F}(x) = \sum_{x_i \leq x} w_i / \sum_i w_i$ and scored with the one-sample
AD statistic
\begin{equation}
A^2 = -n - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} (2j - 1)
\left[\ln u_{(j)} + \ln\!\left(1 - u_{(n+1-j)}\right)\right],
\label{eq:wad}
\end{equation}
which weights the tails through the $\ln u$ and $\ln(1-u)$ factors and whose
null distribution is exactly distribution-free in the sample size $n$. We
calibrate it by Monte Carlo from $u \sim \mathcal{U}[0,1)$ draws of matching $n$
with a fixed seed, so the $p$-value is an exact tail probability rather than a
value read off the \citet{scholz1987} table clamped to $[0.001, 0.25]$; this
replaces the earlier weighted two-sample form, whose \citet{kish1965}
effective-count substitution into the \citet{scholz1987} variance was an
approximation and whose $p$-values saturated as inequalities. The 500
astrometric resampling realizations of the classical comparison reuse one cached
null. The observed comparators are the classical short-GRB offsets of the
\citet{fong2022} BRIGHT census ($N = 80$ after removing the bursts
\citet{skobe2026} reclassify as merger-driven long) and the kilonova-selected
short-GRB ($N = 5$) and long-GRB ($N = 4$) subsamples of \citet{skobe2026}.
The BRIGHT census is maintained as a live catalog whose current release revises
the offset, the redshift, or both for seven of its 83 bursts. We keep the
published values, which are the ones \citet{fong2022} summarize and which later
compilations reproduce; four of the seven revisions are pure redshift updates
that rescale exactly, while the remaining three reflect host associations the
portal documents with neither an uncertainty nor a revised robustness grade.
Adopting every revision instead would move the median observed offset from
$8.4$ to $8.1$~kpc, and adopting only the four that rescale cleanly would move
it to $8.5$~kpc, in both cases far inside the host-association and astrometric
bracket we carry through the comparison.

\subsection{Cosmic-rate integration} \label{sec:cosmicrate}

Intrinsic merger rates are computed with a chunked accumulator around the
\compas\ \fci\ routines. The intrinsic merger-rate density at merger redshift
$z_m$ is the delay-time convolution of the metallicity-specific star-formation
history with the per-system formation efficiency,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}(z_m) = \sum_i \frac{w_i}{p_{\rm draw}\,\langle M_{\rm evolved}\rangle}\,
\mathcal{S}\!\left(Z_i, z_f\right),
\label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
following \citet[their Eq.~1]{neijssel2019}, where the sum runs over \compas\
systems $i$ with STROOPWAFEL weight $w_i$ and birth metallicity $Z_i$, the
formation redshift $z_f$ is fixed by the system delay time $t_{d,i}$ through
$t(z_f) = t(z_m) - t_{d,i}$, the delay time runs from zero-age main sequence to
merger ($t_{\rm form} + t_c$, not the inspiral alone), and $p_{\rm draw}$ is the
metallicity sampling prior. The normalization $\langle M_{\rm evolved}\rangle$
is the \emph{total} star-forming mass the simulated population represents, not a
per-binary mass (\citealt{neijssel2019}, their Sec.~2.1); $w_i$ then carries each
system's share of that population. We take it from the run configuration rather
than fitting it: 53 grid metallicities $\times$ about $10^{6}$ binaries each
\citep[][their Table~1]{broekgaarden2021} $\times$ $79.11\,\Msun$ of Universe
star formation per simulated binary, or $4.19\times10^{9}\,\Msun$. The
per-binary figure follows the \compas\ analytic Kroupa-IMF convention for this
sampling (\citealt{kroupa2001} over $0.01$--$200\,\Msun$, primaries drawn in
$5$--$150\,\Msun$, flat mass ratio with $m_2 \geq 0.1\,\Msun$, binary fraction
corrected to unity; \citealt{broekgaarden2021}, their Sec.~2.2). Because it is a
property of the sampler, one value serves both populations and all 20 models.
The
metallicity-specific star-formation rate density factorizes as
$\mathcal{S}(Z, z) = \mathrm{SFR}(z)\,dP/d\ln Z$. The cosmic star-formation rate
uses the \citet{madau2014} functional form
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SFR}(z) = a\,\frac{(1+z)^b}{1 + [(1+z)/c]^d},
\label{eq:sfr}
\end{equation}
with the IllustrisTNG TNG100-1 best fit $(a, b, c, d) = (0.0172, 1.4425, 4.5299,
6.2261)$ \citep{levina2026}. The metallicity distribution is the
\citet{levina2026} Azzalini skew-log-normal, whose location and scale evolve as
\begin{equation}
\langle Z\rangle(z) = \mu_0\,10^{\mu_z z}, \qquad \omega(z) = \omega_0\,10^{\omega_z z},
\label{eq:mssfr}
\end{equation}
with skewness $\alpha$ and best fit $(\mu_0, \mu_z, \omega_0, \omega_z, \alpha) =
(0.0247, -0.0521, 1.1509, 0.0477, -1.8801)$; $dP/d\ln Z$ is normalized per
natural-log metallicity interval, which we verify numerically. We adopt the
Planck 2015 cosmology of the underlying \fci\
($H_0 = 67.74\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $\Omega_m = 0.3089$,
$\Omega_\Lambda = 0.6911$; \citealt{planck2016}) throughout, to avoid a
high-redshift inconsistency in the rate integral.

The Riemann sum over metallicity in Eq.~\ref{eq:rate} follows the \compas\
\texttt{ClassMSSFR} convention rather than the pointwise lookup in \fci. Interior
bin edges are the log-midpoints between adjacent simulated metallicities, and
the outer edges run to the ends of the metallicity distribution, so the lowest
and highest simulated metallicities absorb the star formation off the ends of
the grid. This matters because the grid stops at $Z = 0.03$ while cosmic star
formation does not: under the \citet{levina2026} TNG100-1 MSSFR, 28.5 per cent
of $z = 0$ star formation lies above that ceiling. The pointwise convention
discards it, which drives $\Rzero$ for BNS a factor 1.48 below the value the
published $w_{000}$ weights in the same files encode, while leaving BHNS, whose
yield has already collapsed by $Z_{\rm max}$, correct to 2 per cent. The cost is
an extrapolation: the top bin applies the $Z = 0.03$ yield to all higher
metallicities, and 26 per cent of the BNS $\Rzero$ we report rests on it. We
quote that fraction with the rate rather than absorbing it.

Together these two choices leave the rate normalization with no free parameter,
and make the published anchors a test instead of an input. Re-running the
\citet{neijssel2019} integration the $w_{000}$ weights were computed with, at
the configuration mass and with this binning, reproduces the stored per-system
weights to $0.4$ per cent for BNS and $0.8$ per cent for BHNS, flat to $0.1$ per
cent across all 100 tabulated redshift slices; the residual is consistent with
the known \fci\ discretization biases. An earlier version of this analysis
instead back-derived $\langle M_{\rm evolved}\rangle$ per population from those
same anchors. That procedure is only sound if the integration is otherwise
correct, and it was not: it absorbed the missing metallicity term above into the
normalization, returning $2.80\times10^{9}\,\Msun$ for BNS (a factor $1.50$
below the configuration mass) against $4.05\times10^{9}\,\Msun$ for BHNS. The
two errors largely cancelled in $\Rzero$, which is why the fault was invisible
there, but the mass also normalizes the formation yields of
Section~\ref{sec:pop}, where it does not cancel. Rates are intrinsic,
comoving, source-frame in $\Gpcyr$ unless stated otherwise. Because the disk-mass
classification assumes 100 percent jet launching above threshold, all GRB rates
are upper bounds \citep{gottlieb2023}.

For cross-checks against the gravitational-wave catalog we also fold detector
selection effects into the same integration. Each per-system term of
Eq.~\ref{eq:rate} is multiplied by the probability that a binary with the
system's component masses, merging at redshift $z$, exceeds a single-detector
signal-to-noise threshold $\rho \geq 8$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}_{\rm det}(z) = \sum_i \frac{w_i}
{p_{\rm draw}\,\langle M_{\rm evolved}\rangle}\,
\mathcal{S}\!\left(Z_i, z_f\right) p_{{\rm det},i}(z).
\label{eq:detrate}
\end{equation}
The detection probability $p_{{\rm det},i}$ interpolates the \fci\
signal-to-noise grids, computed with the IMRPhenomPv2 waveform model
\citep{hannam2014,khan2016} at a representative LIGO O3 sensitivity, and
marginalizes over sky position, inclination, and polarization with the
\citet{finn1993} angular-factor distribution. The expected number of
detections per detector-frame year integrates over the detectable volume,
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm det} = \int_0^{z_{\rm det}} \mathcal{R}_{\rm det}(z)\,
\frac{dV_c}{dz}\,\frac{dz}{1+z},
\label{eq:ndet}
\end{equation}
where the $(1+z)^{-1}$ converts source-frame to detector-frame time and the
integration cap $z_{\rm det} = 1$ (the \fci\ default) lies well beyond the O3
horizon for neutron-star binaries. The local rate of the uncut population
matches the \citet{broekgaarden2021} anchor to under a per cent, but that is now
a result rather than a construction, so $N_{\rm det}$ is a second, independent
check on the same normalization: it stresses the amplitude and shape of $\Rz$
over $z \in [0, 1]$ rather than the $z = 0$ value alone
(Section~\ref{sec:priorwork}).

\subsection{Rate corrections} \label{sec:corrections}

(i) To compare an intrinsic
rate with an observed sGRB rate we multiply by the beaming fraction
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm beam} = 1 - \cos\theta_j, \qquad \mathcal{R}_{\rm obs} = f_{\rm beam}\,\mathcal{R},
\label{eq:beam}
\end{equation}
with per-class jet half-opening angles $\theta_j = 13^\circ$ (range 10 to 16) for
the sbGRB class and $\theta_j = 6.5^\circ$ (range 5 to 8) for the lbGRB classes.
The sbGRB band is a fiducial prior rather than a single measurement: \citet{fong2015}
find a median sGRB half-opening angle $\theta_j = 16^\circ \pm 10^\circ$, while the
\citet{beniamini_nakar2019} structured-jet analysis of GW170817 prefers narrower
cores closer to $10^\circ$, and we adopt $10^\circ$ to $16^\circ$ as the
compromise band.
(ii) BH spin: BHNS rates are computed on the grid
$\aBH \in \{0.0, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$ with a fiducial $\aBH = 0.5$, and
marginalized over a spin prior $p(\aBH)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{R}_{\rm marg} &= \int \mathcal{R}(\aBH)\,p(\aBH)\,d\aBH \\
&\approx \sum_j \mathcal{R}\!\left(\aBH^{(j)}\right) p\!\left(\aBH^{(j)}\right),
\end{split}
\label{eq:spinmarg}
\end{equation}
evaluated on the grid $\{\aBH^{(j)}\}$ for both a flat prior and the
\citet{fuller2019} low-spin prior.
(iii) Spin-orbit misalignment: BHNS population rates are scaled by an
order-unity heuristic factor, $\mathcal{R}_{\rm BHNS} \to 0.5\,\mathcal{R}_{\rm BHNS}$,
motivated by the misaligned-spin distributions of \citet{fragos2010} together
with the numerical-relativity result that the remnant disk mass collapses for
tilt angles beyond $\sim 50$ to $60^\circ$ \citep{kawaguchi2015}; it is applied
at the rate level only, not by dropping systems.
(iv) Jet breakout: the disk-mass BNS class rates are upper bounds that assume
every above-threshold system launches a successful jet; we convert them to
observable rates with the fraction of jets that clear the merger ejecta.
Following \citet{pais2024}, we test per system whether a disk-fed jet breaks out
within the engine working time subject to a collimation gate (their Eqs.~3 and
4) and reduce to a weighted breakout fraction $f_{\rm break}$ per
class. Because the disk mass is knife-edge sensitive to the neutron-star radius
near $R_{1.4}\sim 12$~km, $f_{\rm break}$ is marginalized over the four EOS of
Section~\ref{sec:remnant} and is remnant-conditioned: the prompt-collapse class
is obstructed by the dynamical ejecta alone, while the two HMNS classes also see
a disk wind $f_{\rm wind}\Mdisk$ and a longer launch delay, which lowers their
breakout. The observable rate is the chain
$\mathcal{R} \to f_{\rm break}\,\mathcal{R} \to f_{\rm beam}\,f_{\rm break}\,\mathcal{R}$;
breakout is independent of beaming and of the misalignment factor.
We do not apply beaming or the misalignment factor when comparing against
the GWTC-5.0 intrinsic GW rate, which already folds in selection effects.
Two properties of that reference rate should be kept in view. The interval we
quote is the bottom row of \citet[their Table~2]{abac2026}, the union of the
FullPop and PixelPop $90\%$ credible intervals that they adopt as a conservative
estimate less sensitive to modelling assumptions (their Sec.~4.2); it is a
model-systematics envelope rather than a single posterior interval, and its
coverage under either model alone exceeds $90\%$. Their categories are also
mass-defined at a $2.5\,\Msun$ neutron-star/black-hole split, BNS being
$m_1, m_2 \in [1, 2.5]\,\Msun$ and NSBH $m_1 \in [2.5, 200]\,\Msun$ with
$m_2 \in [1, 2.5]\,\Msun$, whereas our rates are computed after the
cold-stability cut at $\Mtov = 2.2\,\Msun$ (Section~\ref{sec:remnant}). The
neutron stars in $(2.2, 2.5]\,\Msun$ that the mass boxes would count carry
$0.3$ to $10.9\%$ of the BNS weight and $12$ to $52\%$ of the BHNS weight across
the grid ($3.1\%$ and $18.5\%$ in the fiducial model), so our points sit slightly
low relative to a like-for-like definition. The offset is negligible against the
BNS band and deepens rather than relieves the BHNS excess, which makes every
comparison below the conservative one.

\subsection{Observed-sample engine comparison} \label{sec:obsmethod}

To confront the engine branches with data we use the eight kilonova-bearing GRBs
of \citet{rastinejad2024}, the only sample with a uniform blue/purple/red
ejecta decomposition $(M_B, M_P, M_R)$ (their Table~3). For each event we draw
$10^4$ Monte Carlo realizations of the component ejecta masses from split-normal
posteriors and of the burst duration $T_{50}$ (with log-uniform or log-normal
priors for the two events lacking a published $T_{50}$). The black-hole-engine
prediction for the ejecta mass at a given duration follows the
\citet[their Eqs.~10 and 11]{gottlieb2024} scaling
$M_{\rm ej} \propto f^{-1}\,E_{\gamma,\rm iso}\,T_{50}^{\,\alpha-1}$, marginalized
over the jet-structure exponent $\alpha \in [1.5, 2]$ and the ratio
$f_b/\epsilon_\gamma \in [0.02, 0.71]$ set by the \citet{berger2014} beaming
and \citet{beniamini2016} radiative-efficiency bounds. The HMNS-engine
prediction is the thermal disk-wind ejecta $M_{\rm ej} = f_{\rm wind}\,\Mdisk$,
with $f_{\rm wind} \in [0.2, 0.4]$ spanning the GRMHD wind fractions of
\citet{radice2018} and $\Mdisk \in [0.01, 0.10]\,\Msun$
\citep[their Sec.~3.1]{gottlieb2024}. The diagnostic is the residual
$\log_{10}(M_{\rm obs,wind}/M_{\rm pred,BH})$, which is consistent with zero for a
black-hole-disk engine and strongly positive for an HMNS-wind engine. This
comparison is an external consistency check on the engine classes and does not
feed back into the population classification.

$\eta$ counts systems surviving the cold-stability cut, while

rescaling. It removes
23.9 per cent of the BHNS weight at the lowest grid metallicity and none at
the highest (18.5 per cent overall), so the surviving fraction runs from
0.761 to 1.000 across the plotted range; for BNS it is 8.8 per cent, 3.1 per

shifting it, and it acts against the sub-solar preference we report, so that
preference survives but its amplitude is understated by up to a quarter in
the BHNS panel. For the same reason this is not the quantity plotted in the

BHNS-to-BNS yield ratio can be read directly off the figure. An earlier version

their fiducial model). Those two masses differed by a factor 1.45, which we
reported as an unresolved cross-panel systematic and used to justify quoting no
BHNS-to-BNS amplitude ratio. That systematic was an artefact of the rate
normalisation, not a property of the simulations: it was the metallicity-binning
term of Section~\ref{sec:cosmicrate}, which enters the BNS anchor and barely
touches the BHNS one. With the configuration mass in place it is gone, and the
statement it supported is withdrawn.

pooling, as in Fig.~1 of \citet{broekgaarden2022}, and every populated point
is an ordinary point on its curve. Each class carries a distinct marker shape
as well as its palette colour, following the same figure: at this point density
the linestyle alone no longer separates the two lbGRB $+$ red KN classes, which
share the palette red. We
plot no sampling error, following \citet[][their Sec.~2.7]{broekgaarden2021},
who evaluate the STROOPWAFEL sampling uncertainty on this same formation

in their paper in favour of the stellar-evolution and $\mathrm{SFRD}(Z_i,z)$
systematics; their Fig.~5 and Fig.~1 of \citet{broekgaarden2022} likewise
plot every simulated grid point ungated. Their 0.06 per cent is the
population-integrated rate, so it is an argument against drawing a $\sigma$
and not a claim that a single class in a single metallicity bin is as well
sampled. We therefore state the sparsity as raw counts instead. Every BNS
curve is populated at all 53 metallicities; the thinnest point holds 5 systems

curves have one or two empty metallicities each, all of them above $Z_\odot$
and inside the shaded band, where the top two grid points hold five systems in

rather than a measurement. That collapse is \compas's own and not an effect
of our cut: between $Z/Z_\odot=1.39$ and 1.55 the raw number of merging BHNS

rows at all at that step. Fig.~1 of \citet{broekgaarden2022} shows the same
cliff at the same metallicity in all twenty of its BHNS curves; the
difference is that they floor their ordinate at $10^{-8}$, which here would
hide exactly one of the 53 points, the single-system
$4.0\times10^{-10}$ at $Z/Z_\odot=1.90$, which we draw instead.
Drawing the native grid also exposes a factor 2.6
dip in every BNS curve near $Z=0.005$ that the pooling had averaged over. It
is a property of the released weights rather than of this analysis, and it
coincides with a ninefold drop in the number of merging systems the sampler
found at those three metallicities, so we read it as a shortfall in the
STROOPWAFEL refinement there rather than as a metallicity dependence of the
yield. The two Total curves are wide translucent envelopes
rather than classes, since neither is an observable prediction; in the right
panel \textit{No GRB} (the neutron star swallowed whole) carries 82.4 per

\subsection{Population-synthesis robustness} \label{sec:robust}

Across the \citet{broekgaarden2021} grid the local intrinsic rate moves by far
more than the engine demographics, but neither is fixed.
$\Rzero$ spans $0.61$ to $186.6\,\Gpcyr$ for BNS (a factor of $304$, from the
unstable-case-BB model E to the low-kick model Q) and $3.7$ to $397\,\Gpcyr$ for
BHNS, so the absolute rate is not a useful discriminant on its own.
The engine mix is tighter but still turns over: long-duration engines hold the
BNS majority in 13 of the 20 variations and the short-duration, blue-kilonova
class in the other 7 (Figure~\ref{fig:frac20bns}). The formation channel is
informative about the engine but does not determine it: the stable-MT-plus-CE
channel is $81\%$ sbGRB in the fiducial and only $29\%$ in model E, where it
inverts to disk-dominated (Figure~\ref{fig:xtab}). Common-envelope efficiency
is the single strongest lever, carrying the sbGRB fraction from $0.72$ at
$\alpha_{\rm CE} = 0.1$ to $0.31$ at $\alpha_{\rm CE} = 10$
(Figure~\ref{fig:alphace5}), with the mass-transfer-efficiency sweep close
behind (Figure~\ref{fig:betasweep}). The BHNS side is uniform by comparison:
the NS-swallowing No-GRB class is the majority in all 20 models, from $52$ to
$97\%$, and 12 of the 20 exceed the upper edge of the GWTC-5.0 NSBH interval
(Figure~\ref{fig:frac20bhns}), which makes the BHNS rate the tighter constraint
on the binary physics.

Two caveats bound how far the class fractions should be pushed. The dense-matter
EOS is comparable in leverage to the binary physics: at fixed model the
HMNS-engine fraction moves by up to $0.52$ across the four EOS, against up to
$0.61$ across the 20 models at fixed EOS (Figure~\ref{fig:eosmatrix}), so only
an EOS-marginalized statement is defensible. And the long-lived/short-lived
split factor $f_{\rm HMNS}$ is an unsourced heuristic: moving it from the
fiducial $1.20$ to $1.25$ takes the fiducial model's sbGRB fraction from $47.3$
to $58.5\%$ and flips which engine dominates. The engine ordering of any single
model should therefore be read with the grid spread attached, not as a point
prediction.
